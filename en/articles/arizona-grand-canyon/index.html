    Looking quite far back into the past, our first trip abroad together took us to the Mediterranean, to Greece. We returned to the sea a few more times, but at a certain point, we realized that rather than sun baked sand, we were drawn to cooler places: whether it was Scotland, northern North America (the higher, the better), or other northern countries we are still planning to visit. But that doesn’t mean we’ve given up on warm climates entirely, because they have plenty to offer too!

    One of the unlikely destinations you’d expect from lovers of northern places is Arizona. Most people probably associate this southern US state with extreme heat and the Grand Canyon. We went to Arizona to visit family, and even though it’s a corner of the world we probably wouldn't have picked for ourselves, this region not only won us over but definitively confirmed that we probably wouldn’t even need civilization to live.

    A QUICK REFRESHER

    Arizona is located in the southwestern United States. And while we associate the Rocky Mountains with Colorado to the northeast and the Sierra Nevada with sunny California, many might describe Arizona as a sand-filled flatland with a single river that has spent centuries carving out one massive canyon. You won’t find any high mountains there (well, mostly!), yet a large part of the state sits quite high above sea level, giving the landscape a truly unique relief.

    And now, let’s get to the aforementioned canyon. Altitude plays a key role in just how deep and magnificent the Grand Canyon is. The average height of the plateau where the canyon is located ranges from about 1500 to 2500 meters above sea level. In practice, this means that while you might be roasting in fifty degree heat down in the Colorado River bed in summer, up on the rim, you could be surprised by a chilly wind, or in a winter by a meter of snow and a frost you’d expect to find somewhere else entirely.

     

    During our first USA road trip, we saw several national parks, including the Grand Canyon, Yosemite Valley, Sequoia NP, and a quick visit to Zion in Utah. All of them are the crème de la crème of the USA, the absolute peak of what you can see there. Can you imagine trying to pick just one? It’s hard. But if we had to choose, Yosemite made the biggest impression on Bára, while David was awestruck by the immense scale of the Grand Canyon, beyond the reach of cameras and the grasp of the mind.

    A THOUSAND WAYS TO THE CANYON

    When you stand on the edge of the cliff, you can hardly see the Colorado River below. That is how massive this place is. Dust motes dance in the air. The edges of the canyon tower through the haze. And you are just small souls on its brink.

    You can choose to visit the canyon from the north or the south. The southern route is more common, usually from Flagstaff. And if you choose this approach, you will likely be surprised to drive through pine groves instead of a wasteland made only of cacti. For a long time, you have the feeling that you MUST see the canyon already, since it is hundreds of kilometers long and up to thirty wide… but you really don't. You only see the scenery the moment you literally step onto the rim.

    Then there are a few tourists who choose the most interesting way to explore this national park. They don’t just stay at the visitor center and the historic Native American houses, they choose the so called "rim to rim", the trek all the way down to the valley and back up the other side. We settled for a day at the top, where the high altitude made it pleasant (almost cold), with incredible views and beautiful nature. We are sure that descending to the bottom must be an even more unforgettable experience. What surprised us was that even in October, it must have been close to 40 degrees Celsius down there.

    ARIZONA ISN'T JUST THE GRAND CANYON

    After a few road trips, we recommend visiting national parks out of season. During the peak, it is one big tourist massacre. Parking lots burst at the seams, and you will most likely find yourself waiting in a long queue just to be let into the park at all. The Grand Canyon is undoubtedly the state’s biggest attraction, but it is far from the only one. Just leave the towering three-thousand-meter peaks behind and drive down from Flagstaff to the significantly lower elevations of the south, where a hellish heat will hit you instead of a cool breeze. We found Sedona in "hell"... but maybe more on that next time.
