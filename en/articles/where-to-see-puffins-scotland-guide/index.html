    Puffins are essentially like little sailors. Did you know that they spend most of their lives alone on the high seas, where they even sleep directly on the waves? To us, that idea seems almost unimaginable. They only return to land to breed. The fact that they spend most of their time on the water also means they are excellent flyers (they reportedly flap their wings up to 400 times per minute!), and perhaps even better divers. And another fun fact: they can dive to depths of up to 60 meters for fish! At least, that’s what we’ve heard.

    Taking all of the above into account, it might seem difficult to encounter them. Your only chance is usually between April and the end of August, while they return to land. Then they disappear back to the ocean. So, if you want to see them, you have a fairly limited window of time.

    Where to look for them in Scotland?

    Puffins love peace and quiet, so they nest primarily on remote cliffs and islands. Here are our tips:

    • Isle of May (Firth of Forth): A great choice if you are in Edinburgh and don’t want to travel too far.
    • Isle of Staffa: A beautiful place thanks to its basalt columns and the massive Fingal’s Cave.
    • Lunga (Treshnish Isles): Our favorite. Basically the puffin capital.
    • North Coast: If you are doing a road trip in the north (like the NC500 route), keep an eye on the cliffs, you might get lucky there too!

    Our experience: A trip with Staffa Tours

    We set out to find them on the Isle of Lunga, and it was one of the TOP trips we’ve taken in Scotland during our years of visiting. We sailed from the Isle of Mull with Staffa Tours, and the boat journey itself is a massive experience.

    It’s quite a trek from Tobermory to Staffa and Lunga, so you’ll spend several hours on the water. Our captain was great and knew that besides the puffins themselves, the crew would be interested in marine life. Practically the whole time, he was actively trying to find anything from whales and seals to dolphins. Thanks to him, you don’t just sail boringly from "point A to point B", but you get to enjoy a proper sea safari.

    Before we reached Lunga, we stopped at Fingal’s Cave on the Isle of Staffa. The cave (and the entire island) is completely unique because it is made of perfect hexagonal basalt columns that look as if someone laid them out with a ruler. The cave also has an incredible echo, which is why it’s called the "Cave of Melodies" and if you’re lucky enough to have calm seas, you can even go inside.

    We didn’t manage to land on Staffa because the local pier required repairs after previous storms. However, even just looking into that dark cave entrance and hearing the thundering of the crashing waves makes you feel like you’re looking into the throat of some prehistoric beast. And when you look closer, you’ll find mossy and grassy spots around the edges where heather blooms. And, of course, you’ll see puffins flying everywhere around you.

    Practical travel tip

    We spent the entire journey on the upper part of the boat, which is open and significantly exposed to the wind. Yes, you are still in Scotland, and if there is one thing you can expect, it is wind and rain. Proper layering of clothes is more than necessary, and a hat is a must. Just watch out for caps, we definitely don’t recommend them! They would stay on your head for maybe five minutes in that wind. Another practical tip is binoculars. Both on Staffa and Lunga, you will be literally just a few steps away from the puffins, but you still can't go everywhere, so the binoculars came in really handy, even during the search for wildlife during the voyage itself.

    Once the boat anchors at Lunga and you climb up the cliff, a completely different world opens up before you. The untouched nature is incredible (which is also why you can’t do whatever you want on the island, like flying a drone). Seeing colonies of nesting puffins this close is priceless. Generally, they bustle around their burrows in the ground, but occasionally one will fly right over your head with a beak full of small fish, and they usually ignore you completely. It's like watching an endless theater performance.
